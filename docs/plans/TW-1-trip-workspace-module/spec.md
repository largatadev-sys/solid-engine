# TW-1 — The Trip Workspace module: the god module's trip half moves, and the workspace gets its own grammar

**Status:** drafted 2026-09-07 — awaiting the owner's review and `/to-tickets`; the build starts only after the content-module boundaries story (epic map, *Content-module boundaries — api-only between diary, postcard and publication*) has merged, by founder ruling at this pass · **Epic:** none (the content-remodel arc, after CM-2) · **Depends on:** CM-1 (merged — the `trip` facade, `trip.api`'s five records, `DELETE /v1/trips/{id}`), CM-2 (merged — the content readers cut over, so the trip half is what is left to move), the content-module boundaries story (not started — it chooses the guard form, the layout threshold and the event mechanism every module shares; this spec names what it must re-read from there) · **Branch:** `feature/TW-1-trip-workspace-module`.
**Grilled:** the design was grilled 2026-08-30 → 31 (grill-with-docs, two rounds; the record is `design.md` beside this file, now the background this spec restates rather than incorporates). Nine rulings taken at the 2026-09-07 planning pass amend it and are listed first under *Implementation Decisions*; the design's own rejected options stay rejected.
**ADR:** **ADR-037** (new, minted with this story) — the `/v1/trips/**` grammar for the whole workspace, reversing the 2026-08-30 declination recorded on the epic map's restructure line and in `08-object-contracts.md`; ADR-008 upheld, because every old path is served by the same handler and never sunsets. The ADR-002 amendment the design owes (api-only, no entity crosses, schema per module recorded, no transaction across a line) is appended to the content-boundaries story's ADR if it wrote one, else minted here as ADR-038.
**Candidate-capability note:** none — the story adds no traveler act; every act that exists today exists afterward at a second address.
**Freshness note:** no surface changes lane. The workspace stays live over the `itinerary:{id}:{channel}` topics, whose grammar and payloads (`itineraryId`) do not move — the REST grammar changes, the transport grammar does not; the Trips list stays on its traveler topic; the two content readers this story touches (preview, published) stay focus-fresh pull.

## Problem Statement

The trip's code lives in the wrong place and answers at the wrong address. `com.largata.itinerary` is the 135-file god module the strangler is digesting: its content half moved out at CM-1 and CM-2, but its trip half — the record, the plan, the editing session, the cover, the dump, the lifecycle acts — is still there, flat, with 13 controllers in one folder and 42 DTOs in another. The `trip` module that postcard and publication already depend on is a raw-SQL facade over that half's tables, kept alive by a strangler waiver whose dissolution condition is this story. Workspace and membership, which the trip cannot be created without, are two more packages that reach into it and are reached into. Nothing can be moved alone, nothing is sealed, and the only guard that holds the line is a regex that forbids the new world from naming the old.

On the wire, the trip is the one object with no address of its own: postcards, diaries and publications answer at `/v1/postcards`, `/v1/diaries`, `/v1/publications`, and the trip answers at `/v1/itineraries`, a noun ADR-013 froze before the vocabulary settled. The founder ruled at this pass that the trip becomes the itinerary going forward, on the wire as well as in the code.

## Solution

Move the trip half of the god module into `com.largata.trip` as one bounded context — trip, plan, editing, history, cover, workspace, ownership, fork — organized as feature slices with an `api` the other modules import and nothing else. Give every workspace route a second address under `/v1/trips/**`, served by the same handler as its `/v1/itineraries/**` original, so the two grammars cannot drift: one Java method, two paths, and a test that enumerates the mappings and names any route without its twin. Retire the facade: its reads become the api's implementations over the module's own repositories, and the waiver dissolves for the trip's tables. Cut the mobile client's five workspace repositories over to the new grammar with no screen change. Leave the itinerary package standing with its content half, every old path answering byte for byte, and its decommissioning a later story.

## User Stories

1. As a traveler on the shipped app, I want every workspace screen to keep working through this story, so that a refactor I cannot see costs me nothing.
2. As a traveler on an older app version, I want `/v1/itineraries/**` to answer exactly as before, so that I am never forced to update mid-trip.
3. As a traveler on the new app version, I want the same screens to talk to `/v1/trips/**`, so that the trip is addressed as what it is.
4. As a traveler editing a plan with a co-traveler, I want the editing-session and plan-saved frames to arrive as they do today, so that the live workspace is untouched by where the code lives.
5. As a trip owner, I want publishing from either grammar to do the same thing, so that the address I used never decides what "published" means.
6. As a trip owner, I want a publish to leave the publication object the new world reads from, so that the two worlds agree on what is published.
7. As a member accepting an invitation or a join link, I want admission to stay one act with the invitation's status flip, so that a failed admission never leaves an accepted invitation with no member.
8. As a trip owner archiving a trip, I want pending invitations voided and the ownership offer withdrawn as today, so that an archived trip stops recruiting.
9. As the next agent, I want the trip module to have one door — its `api` and its refusals — and a guard that fails the build on any other import, so that I cannot reach into it by accident.
10. As the next agent, I want the trip's code in feature slices whose folder says what the code is about, so that I find the editing session by looking for `editing/`.
11. As the next agent, I want a test that tells me the moment an old-grammar route has no `/v1/trips` twin, or a twin served by a different method, so that the grammars cannot drift silently.
12. As the next agent, I want the old-world integration tests to pass with package edits only and zero assertion edits, so that the move is proven by the suites it did not write.
13. As the next agent, I want the guards' temporary migration windows gone before the PR, with a test that says so, so that a branch-local exemption cannot outlive the branch.
14. As the next agent, I want the content half of the itinerary package to read the trip through its api and never its entities, so that the package can be relocated or deleted later without touching trip.
15. As the founder, I want nothing from the itinerary package deleted and the old grammar verified before the package is decommissioned, so that the rollback path is the old address, not a revert.
16. As the founder, I want every deferral named with its trigger, so that the schema move and the FK drops are decisions on the record rather than things forgotten.

## Implementation Decisions

**The nine rulings of 2026-09-07, which amend `design.md` and are not re-argued:**
1. **Wire grammar: mint `/v1/trips/**` for the whole workspace** — the record, plan days and activities, edit-lock, plan save, photo dump, cover, lifecycle acts, publish and unpublish, fork, preview, and the trip-rooted routes other modules own (members and ownership offers, invitations, polls, chat, join link and join requests). This reverses the 2026-08-30 ruling that declined a parallel trip-path grammar. ADR-008 holds: no old path, shape, status code or semantic moves, because the twin is the same handler.
2. **Module unit: the design's.** `workspace` (11 files) and `membership` (8, including ownership) absorb into `trip`. Chat, poll, invitation and join stay outside and reach trip through its api.
3. **Nothing is deleted from the itinerary package.** Its trip half migrates by `git mv`; its content half — the old diary-entry controllers, the published-itinerary readers, discovery, the postcard feed, the public-profile readers, roughly 35 files and 7 of the 13 controllers — stays where it is, out of scope, reading the trip through the api. Decommissioning is a separate story with its own trigger.
4. **Sequencing: the content-module boundaries story lands first**, as the epic map records. Its rulings on the guard form, the layout threshold and the event mechanism bind this story; see *Inputs owed*.
5. **Coexistence: the code moves once; the old paths are adapters by construction.** One implementation, both grammars, no second mapping over any table. The alternative — a copied implementation running beside the old one — was declined for the lost-update hazard of two JPA mappings on one row and for landing every fix twice.
6. **The content half is served through trip's api**, not a legacy exemption. The design's recorded time-boxed exemption for `com.largata.itinerary` is set aside: the api gets real consumers now, and discovery, feed and profile are live surfaces that will need exactly these reads when they relocate to their own modules.
7. **Publish is merged: `POST /v1/trips/{id}/publish` flips the trip's flag and mints the publication object**, and the client moves to it. The mechanism is under *Publish* below.
8. **The move is sliced behind branch-local migration windows** in both guards, each sabotage-checked, both deleted before the PR by a named ticket.
9. **This spec is written before the content-boundaries story**, so that story's grilling chooses the module conventions knowing the largest module's shape.

**What "nothing deleted" means here, stated so it can be objected to at review:** files move. The only deletions are CM-1 scaffolding the real implementation replaces — the raw-SQL facade `trip/service/TripService`, the dark `TripResponse` on `GET /v1/trips/{id}`, publication's two dark publish routes (superseded by ruling 7), `itinerary/TripsTopic` (which becomes a listener in `ws`), the guard exemption that named the facade, and the two migration windows.

**The layout — eight feature slices, one `api`, class names unchanged.** After the move the module holds roughly 78 files: `api/` (the three interfaces, CM-1's five records kept by name, the view records, the events, and `PublishedTripQueries` for the content half), `exception/` (only what an outside caller catches), and the slices `trip/`, `plan/`, `editing/`, `history/`, `cover/`, `workspace/`, `ownership/`, `fork/`, plus `validation/` for the seven DTO validators (measured: used only by trip's DTOs, so `common` is not needed). Under the allowlist guard form the slices sit at the module root; under the marker form they nest one level under `internal/`; nothing else in this document changes between the two. Controllers live inside their slice with both prefixes. `Itinerary`, `ItineraryService`, `ItineraryController`, `ItineraryResponse` keep their names — the move is a move, and renames belong to the parked vocabulary-housekeeping line. Visibility widens where a slice line is crossed and the ArchUnit guard becomes the seal, the same price `refactor(postcard)` paid at CM-2. `TripLifecycle` absorbs `ItineraryState` (same three names, same `wireName()`, storage unchanged and pinned). Fork moves whole: its act writes five trip tables in one transaction, so it is a trip act, and `fork_relationship` survives destruction exactly as today.

**The adapter mechanic — one controller, both prefixes.** Every trip-owned controller declares `{"/v1/itineraries/{id}/…", "/v1/trips/{id}/…"}` at class level; the other modules add the second path to their existing trip-rooted mappings, one line each in poll, chat, join and invitation. `TripMembershipController` splits: the members and ownership-offer routes move into `trip/ownership/` because they call the membership service; the invitation routes stay in invitation. Two collisions are resolved by construction: `GET /v1/trips/{id}` answers `ItineraryResponse` from the unified handler and CM-1's dark `TripResponse` retires; `DELETE /v1/trips/{id}` stays alone in its own controller under the new prefix only, because a method-level mapping on the unified controller would mint `DELETE /v1/itineraries/{id}`, which S4.38 left unminted on purpose — the absence is asserted. Two routes leave the unified controller: `GET /{id}/preview` calls a content-half service and answers a content DTO, so it moves to a new `ItineraryPreviewController` in the staying half with both prefixes; the retired verbs `audience` and `finish-planning` get no twin and are named in the twin test's exclusion set.

**The api, in three interfaces, no god service.** `TripApi` (facts, teasers, titles, state, frozen, share-card version, the published-among and count reads the profile needs, day counts, fork provenance), `PlanApi` (the plan, the published plan fenced by `PublishedVisibility`, a day, an activity, an activity snapshot), `MembershipApi` (membership, roster, owner, `admit`, workspace ids, archived state, owned trips, trips in sight). Each is implemented by the slice that owns the answer; the ten existing services stay ten. CM-1's record names are kept where postcard and publication already consume them. Every consumer moves to the api: postcard and publication off the facade, invitation and join off `ItineraryService` and `WorkspaceService`, poll and ws off `WorkspaceService`.

**Events, and the transport that stops being a dependency.** Six events live in `trip.api`: `MembershipArrived` (moves from invitation and gains `tripId`, so the ws listener stops looking it up), `MembershipEnded` (moves from membership), `TripArchived` and `TripUnarchived` (new — they replace the archive act's direct call into invitation with an `ArchiveVoidsInvitations` listener there), `EditingSessionChanged` and `PlanSaved` (new — `itinerary/TripsTopic` imports `ws.EventFanout`, which trip may not name, so trip publishes and a `ws/TripsTopicListener` broadcasts on the same topic with the same type strings and field order; the frames are byte-identical and the unedited ws ITs prove it). This is ADR-030 decision 3 applied as written. Whether the listeners fire AFTER_COMMIT or BEFORE_COMMIT, and what happens when one fails, is the content-boundaries story's ruling; this story adopts it. The topic grammar `itinerary:{id}:{channel}` and its `itineraryId` payloads do not move.

**Publish, merged** *(ruling 7; mechanism owed at review — G-2)*. Today two acts exist: the old path runs the full act (owner and editing-session checks, the flag, analytics) and answers `ItineraryResponse`; CM-1's dark route in publication mints the object and flips the flag bare. Recommended: trip owns the act on both prefixes and publishes `TripPublished` and `TripUnpublished`; publication reacts by minting, refreshing or retiring the object. One semantics, one shape, one handler; the client's change is a path string; the facade's `markPublished`/`markUnpublished` — a cross-module write inside publication's transaction that the design's rule 3 forbids — is deleted rather than recorded; publication's two dark routes and their contract tests are rewritten to drive publish through the trip path and assert the object. The reaction's failure mode (flag flipped, object missing) is harmless while the object has no live reader, and BEFORE_COMMIT closes it at the cost of a cross-module write. The alternative keeps the route in publication calling a new `TripApi.publish`, which leaves the old path flip-only, the grammars different in shape, and the client's mutation unable to consume the body.

**The facade retires; destruction keeps its reach.** The facade's reads become the api's repository-backed implementations. `destroy` moves verbatim into a `TripDestructionService` and keeps its five deletes on other modules' tables (poll, invitation, join request, join link, chat message): an event-driven destruction is impossible before the FK drops, since invitation, poll and join carry `NOT NULL REFERENCES workspace`, and the one-transaction contract is pinned. This is the one place trip names another module's table after this story; it is recorded as debt with the FK-drop story as its trigger. The guard's exemption that named the facade is dropped.

**Deferred, recorded, each with a trigger.** The schema move (`SET SCHEMA trip`, design rule 2): no named schema exists anywhere, CM-1's tables sit in `public`, and every inbound FK would become cross-schema on the day the design's own measured `pg_dump` trap applies — trigger: the FK-drop story or the first extraction rehearsal. The FK drops (`poll → workspace`, `poll_vote → membership`, join's, invitation's): each needs a stepping test and a service replacement for the cascade — with the schema move. `MembershipApi.admit` in its own transaction (rule 3): a pinned test says admission and the invitation's status flip roll back together — recorded, not applied. No migration ships in this story; V55 stays head.

**Mobile — six repository files, zero screens.** `itineraryRepository.ts` becomes `tripRepository.ts` with every function on `/v1/trips/…` except `fetchPublished`, which stays on the content half's `/v1/published-itineraries/{id}`. The four workspace repositories (invitation, join, poll, chat) repoint their trip-rooted functions; `diaryRepository.ts` is content and stays whole. `itineraryKeys` keeps its name and values: it is the object the websocket handlers write into, and renaming it inside the story that rewires the HTTP layer joins two risks for no wire benefit. Two structural guards in the layering test's style: no repository names `/v1/itineraries` except the diary one, and every cache write in the trip-events handler goes through an imported key factory. The 45 end-to-end spec files that speak the old grammar stay unedited as the old world's proof; the seed helper moves to the new grammar so every web walk drives it; one new API-lane spec walks the workspace end to end on `/v1/trips`; the seven scripts repoint.

**Vocabulary and docs**: ADR-037; the ADR-002 amendment appended or minted; the epic map's restructure line annotated with this story, the TW-1 line marked pulled with the amended rulings, the content-boundaries line's trigger left as recorded; `08-object-contracts.md`'s wire table rewritten (`/v1/trips/**` is the workspace grammar; `/v1/itineraries/**` never sunsets and is served by the same handlers); the Trip and Trip Workspace glossary entries; BUILD_STATUS gets this story's row at the gate.

## Testing Decisions

A good test here asserts **what a path answers, what rows exist afterward, and which handler served it** — never a module's internals. Six seams, four of which exist.

1. **The old-world suites, unedited in every assertion** *(the proof that the move moved nothing)* — the 56 integration-test classes under itinerary and the 18 under workspace and membership take package and import edits only; the count with zero assertion edits is stated at the gate. Hibernate's `ddl-auto: validate` at the first context boot proves the nine tables unchanged.
2. **Two grammar tests on the existing shared context signature** *(new; no new signature, because the suite already runs 26 signatures against a 100-connection ceiling)* — a twin test that reads the handler mapping, collects every `(method, pattern)` under `/v1/itineraries`, and asserts a `/v1/trips` twin with the same method and the same handler method, minus a named exclusion set, plus the pinned absence of `DELETE /v1/itineraries/{id}`; and an equivalence test that calls both grammars with one bearer and diffs the bodies byte for byte, including the masking cases. Each is sabotage-checked once by hand and its failure line read: a prefix removed from a controller, a fake delete mapping under the old prefix, one response field flipped.
3. **The guards, in their final form** *(prior art: the four per-module ArchUnit guards and `NewWorldBoundaryTest`)* — trip's guard in the boundaries story's form with four sabotage checks (module seen, front door non-empty, sealed predicate non-empty, a slice list asserted to name real packages); the old-world regex shrunk twice with its positive and negative cases updated; the migration windows sabotage-checked while they exist and asserted absent once closed; a source-text test that the itinerary package never names `com.largata.trip.` outside `api` and `exception`.
4. **The ws frame tests, unedited** *(prior art: `EditingSessionEventsIT`, `TripListEventsIT`)* — they pin that the listener's frames are byte-identical to the old topic's.
5. **Playwright, API lane** *(prior art: `e2e/api/lifecycle.spec.ts`)* — one new spec walks the workspace on `/v1/trips` end to end; the 45 old-grammar specs stay unedited as the old world's proof, and the seed helper's move to the new grammar makes every web walk drive it.
6. **Jest for the two client guards** *(prior art: `layering.test.ts`)* — each with a "would fire" case.

What no seam reaches and the gate closes by hand, on the LAN rung with `t1 = owner, t2 = member`: create, a day and an activity under the editing session, save, cover, invite by handle, accept, chat, a poll and a vote, a join link, archive and unarchive, publish and unpublish — with the backend log read for `/v1/trips` requests and for zero `/v1/itineraries` requests except the diary and published-itineraries reads.

## Out of Scope

A destruction screen (`DELETE /v1/trips/{id}` stays dark; S4.38's Delete-is-archive ruling stands) · the schema move · the FK drops · the publication readers' cutover from `/v1/published-itineraries` to `/v1/publications` · relocating or deleting the itinerary package's content half · renaming classes, tables or query keys · the content-side half of the boundaries work (postcard's hold on diary's entities, `DiaryDeleted`) · Spring Modulith's `verify()` (arrives when the god module is gone) · the Home feed's diary card and the workspace day's "Post to diary".

## Further Notes

- **Measured before this was written, so the ticket order rests on facts, not the design's numbers:** four main files outside the god module import it (invitation, join twice, membership) naming five classes; eight files of the content half still reach into the trip half (`ItineraryRepository` in six, the `Itinerary` entity in six, `DayRepository` and `ActivityRepository` in two each, `ItineraryService` in one) — CM-2 cut the diary readers over and left discovery, feed, profile and the published page as they were; `ItineraryService` reaches the other way once, for a showcase DTO, and sheds it; `WorkspaceService` publishes invitation's event and `MembershipService` calls invitation's service, two cycles the design did not list; no raw SQL names a trip table outside itinerary and trip; the seven validators are used only by trip's DTOs.
- **The cycles decide the order.** Membership imports the itinerary service, the lease service and invitation's service; the itinerary service forms the workspace; the controller needs membership; editing needs plan's repositories and plan needs editing; cover needs editing and history. Workspace is the only slice that can move alone once `MembershipArrived` lives in `trip.api`. Everything after it is welded, which is why the windows exist: without them the move is one commit touching around 120 files.
- **The migration windows are the one temporary exemption this story tolerates**, and only because they are named, sabotage-checked, deleted by a ticket, and asserted gone by a test. The design's permanent legacy exemption for the content half was set aside at ruling 6.
- **The connection ceiling is a constraint, not a note:** 26 Spring context signatures at four connections each already sit at the container's limit. Every new integration test in this story reuses the shared signature; any guard or twin test that needs no context is a plain unit test.
- **The story ends the two guarantees CM-1 was built around**, as the design priced: it is the first story to touch live code, and pre-existing tests are edited — package lines only, counted at the gate.
- **`design.md` is background.** Where this spec and the design differ, the nine rulings above win; where this spec is silent, the design's reasoning stands.
