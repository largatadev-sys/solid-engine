## Problem Statement

A traveler who publishes a trip today gets a page that is the trip's live plan seen through a flag, not a thing of its own. Discover, the Home feed, the profile showcase, the join card and the workspace fence all read that flag; the Itinerary object CM-1 minted for exactly this purpose is dark, called by nothing, and the two publish acts wear one verb. What follows for travelers: the page cannot outlive the trip, a share link is really a trip id, a fork copies whatever the owner has since edited rather than what the forker read, and a private owner's Itinerary would be refused by the dark object read on a rule the founder never made. What follows for the build: the old package still holds the readers — 40 files, 17 live beans, 22 mappings on seven roots, 39 integration tests — reaching into the trip module through a counted exemption whose only purpose is to let them be deleted rather than rewritten; the object's plan is one text blob nothing can filter; the module that owns the Itinerary cannot take its name because the old package holds it; and the fork move ADR-039 attached here would, done as TW-1 left it, create the first cycle in the dependency graph.

## Solution

Make the Itinerary the thing every public surface reads. Publishing creates or refreshes an Itinerary with its own address; the trip's record is untouched and its workspace is fenced for as long as a live Itinerary exists, exactly as today, with "published" now meaning that and nothing else. The page, Discover, Home and the profile read Itineraries, keyed by the Itinerary's id. Forking copies the Itinerary the forker is reading. Every signed-in traveler may read any Itinerary. Then the old package goes: its readers replaced by three composition modules, its diary half relocated into `postcard` unchanged, the module that owns the Itinerary renamed for it, the old wire root sunset but for eight diary routes, every guard that named the old world dissolved, and the module boundary enforced by the build. No backfill: a trip published by the old flag and never republished reads as unpublished, and editable, from the day the readers move, and its owner republishes in one tap.

## User Stories

1. As a trip owner, I want publishing to create an Itinerary with its own address, so that what I share is a thing of its own and not a view of my workspace.
2. As a trip owner, I want my workspace fenced while my Itinerary is live, exactly as today, so that a co-traveler cannot edit the plan out from under the people reading it.
3. As a trip owner, I want unpublishing to take the Itinerary down and give me my workspace back, so that unpublish means what it says.
4. As a trip owner, I want republishing to bring the same address back with the plan as it now is and to rise on Discover as a new publication, so that a link I shared once is never dead for good and a changed plan is seen as changed.
5. As a trip owner, I want the workspace to keep showing me "Published", the published-page shortcut and Unpublish, so that the rebuild changes nothing I look at every day.
6. As a trip owner whose trip was published before this story, I want to find it unpublished and editable with one tap to republish, so that the cutover costs me one act and no data.
7. As a trip owner archiving a published trip, I want its page and card hidden until I unarchive, so that archive keeps the meaning it has today.
8. As a trip owner, I want hard-deleting my Itinerary to unfreeze my trip, so that the two states never disagree.
9. As any signed-in traveler, I want to read any Itinerary, private profile or not, so that "published" means public.
10. As a traveler on Discover, I want the cards, the count, the trending destinations, the recommendations and the suggestions to show Itineraries, so that what I find is what the owner published.
11. As a traveler on Discover, I want the count and the list to agree under every filter, so that "12 itineraries" is twelve I can open.
12. As a traveler on Home, I want a postcard's trip link to open the Itinerary, so that the feed and the page agree.
13. As a traveler on a profile, I want the published count, the destination count and the showcase to reflect the owner's live Itineraries, so that the profile and Discover tell one story.
14. As a traveler forking, I want my new trip made from the Itinerary I am reading, so that the copy is what I saw and not a plan its owner has since changed.
15. As a traveler forking, I want the new trip's provenance to name the Itinerary, so that attribution survives the source trip's destruction.
16. As a traveler holding an old share link, I want it to keep opening the page for a while, so that the change of identity is invisible to me.
17. As a traveler viewing an Itinerary's photos, I want them to load whenever the page does, so that a page is never a page with holes.
18. As a traveler on any of the five old Trip Diary screens, I want them to keep working unchanged, so that a story about publishing does not break my diary.
19. As the founder, I want the readers rebuilt and walked on `dev` while the old ones still answer, and the deletion to be a separate revertable unit, so that the only running environment never carries both risks at once.
20. As the founder, I want to be told in the PR that `dev`'s published set goes dark on cutover until the seeders run, so that I do not read it as a regression.
21. As the next agent, I want the old package gone, the module named for what it owns, every guard that named the old world dissolved, the graph acyclic by rule and Modulith's `verify()` green, so that the strangler is finished and not merely quiet.
22. As the next agent, I want Discover, Home and the profile each in a module of its own with no table, so that a read surface's logic has a home that is not a content object.
23. As the next agent, I want the contracts the old tests proved re-proven by new tests before the old ones die, so that nothing the readers promised is lost in the move.

## Implementation Decisions

**The rulings, from the grilling record.** The freeze stays whole (round 1 Q1 b). The trip record keeps `published` and `publishedAt` and gains a nullable `itineraryId`, all read through a port (Q2). The object id is the public identity (Q3). No backfill and no read-fallback (Q4 c); the flag columns stop being written and stay in place (round 2 Q8). Discover filters on denormalized columns (Q5). The old diary half relocates into `postcard` (Q6). Two PRs (Q7). A `profile` composition module (Q8). Photos stay in media (Q9). Nothing renames in the schema (Q10). The glossary says the record is untouched and the workspace fenced (round 2 Q1). Fork acts on the object; old fork rows are left (Q2). Legacy links resolve by trip then rewrite (Q3). The page read widens additively and the snapshot gains the pin and photo ids (Q4). Archive hides the page (Q6). Republish refreshes `publishedAt` (Q7). The Itinerary's audience is every signed-in traveler, ADR-034 as ruled; CM-1's visibility fence on the object read is a contradiction and comes off (round 3). Signed-in only; the anonymous page is an epic-map line (round 4).

**"Published" means a live Itinerary exists.** The publication-state port that the write fence, chat, invitations and join links already consult is re-implemented by the itinerary module: true when an unretired Itinerary exists for the trip. Nothing that consults it changes, so plan edits, membership changes, joining and chat are fenced exactly as today, and retiring or hard-deleting the Itinerary is what unfreezes the trip. A second read on the same port — the live Itinerary's id and its `publishedAt` — feeds the trip record's three fields. The port stays in the shared kernel until the dissolution ADR-039 scheduled after this story. `trip` never imports `itinerary` (ADR-039 decision 3); the cycle rule that lands ahead of this story is the proof.

**The itinerary module.** The publication module is renamed `itinerary` in the second PR, once the old package is gone; it works under its working name until then. It owns: the Itinerary and its table; publish and unpublish on the trip's grammar as CM-3 left them, with the editing-session guard and the tolerant unpublish; preview, moved from the old package and answering the page's shape; the page read on the Itinerary's id, widened additively with the creator card, fork count, provenance, estimated cost, pin and typed days with their activities and photo ids, beside the raw plan document; a by-trip read that answers the trip's live Itinerary in the page's shape, used by legacy links and by the owner's "view published"; fork on the Itinerary's id, which builds a Trip from the snapshot through a trip-creation port and writes provenance naming the Itinerary; hard delete as CM-1 built it. **Audience, everywhere in this module: every signed-in traveler** (ADR-034 decision 2), masked as not found for non-owners while the trip is archived (today's posture), not found when retired or absent. The visibility fence in the object read is removed. **The table gains** title, destination, duration in days and cover URL, written at mint and refresh and indexed for Discover; **the snapshot gains** the pin and per-activity photo ids at mint. The five Itineraries already on `dev` predate both and render without them until republished. Republish refreshes the snapshot, the columns and `publishedAt` under the same id. The plan api and the editing-session port stay the module's only way into the trip.

**Three composition modules, no table, no SQL, paths unchanged.** Each is classified under ADR-038's rule at birth with an allowlist guard, and each composes at least three apis, which is what makes it a module rather than a Middle Man (ADR-039 decision 4). *`discovery`* owns the Discover routes: browse and count through one itinerary api call whose SQL predicate answers both, so the count agrees with the list (the bug on epic-map line 193 is what this rule closes); trending destinations and recommended over the same api; suggestions over Itinerary titles and destinations; people over identity's search. The archived set and the hidden-owner set are passed *into* the query, never post-filtered. *`feed`* owns the Home feed routes: the postcard api's paged query by author set, as today's adapter already does; author cards from identity; the `following` scope from identity's follow set; hidden private authors excluded in the query; the card's trip link resolved to the **Itinerary's id** through the publication api. *`profile`* owns the traveler profile routes, the follower and following lists, and the caller's own profile stats and showcase: the header and follow lists from identity, the published and destination counts and the showcase from the itinerary module's owner-scoped reads, cards carrying the Itinerary's id. Per-object lists stay where they are, the diaries list in `diary` being the precedent. The filter model, the cursor grammar and the response records move with their surface and keep their shapes.

**The old diary half relocates into `postcard`.** The old diary-entry service, entity, repository and photo audience, its request and response records, its two controllers, the profile's per-trip diary list and the feed's per-trip-by-author page are already adapters over postcard's legacy-entries port; they move into `postcard` as a legacy adapter set with every path and shape unchanged, and the port folds back inside the module that owned it. The old entry table and its photo subject stay. The five client screens that call these paths are untouched. Their cutover and the adapters' deletion are the epic-map line minted from this grilling, with CM-2's original trigger.

**The cutover, and what `dev` looks like the next morning.** No migration touches the flag. The morning the first PR lands, every trip published by the old act and never republished has no Itinerary: its page answers not found, it leaves Discover, its owner's workspace shows it unpublished and **editable again**, and one tap on Publish mints its Itinerary from the plan as it is. On `dev` that is roughly 290 trips including the seeded demo set, and Discover shows the handful of real Itineraries until the seeders run through the new route. The PR description says this rather than letting the founder discover it as a regression.

**The client.** Publish and unpublish move onto the itinerary module's routes and both mutations refetch rather than write the response into the trip's cache, the shape CM-3 built and then deferred (the commit is named in Further Notes). The published-page read moves onto the Itinerary's id; on a miss the page screen tries the by-trip read and rewrites its own URL to the Itinerary's id. Fork moves onto the Itinerary's route. The workspace's badge, settings items and row tap target keep reading the trip record's published fields; the row, the share link and "view published" navigate by its new `itineraryId`. Discovery cards and feed trip links carry the Itinerary's id and navigate as before. The client learns nothing new about diaries. The Playwright suites, the seed helper and the scripts that name the five retiring acts move to the new routes or lose those steps; the occurrences that name diary entries stay. The query keys and hook names are **not** renamed here (an owed ruling, below).

**Media.** Photos referenced by a live Itinerary stay workspace media under the trip's media audience, which consults the publication-state port instead of the flag: readable by every signed-in traveler while the Itinerary is live and the trip not archived, exactly today's rule. Destruction leaving a page's photos dangling is accepted and recorded; copying into the Itinerary's custody is the evolution, with destruction's screen as its trigger.

**The second PR: deletion, rename, sunset.** In order, each a ticket. The new modules' integration tests exist and are green, written in the first PR. The old package's non-diary files and their integration tests are deleted, the row-backed publication state with them. The publication module is renamed `itinerary` and its guard and contract tests follow. The old root sunsets: every controller's dual root collapses to the trip's grammar, the five old-root lifecycle acts die, the projection root goes, the twin and equivalence tests retire having done their job, and the refusal code on the new root flips to `TRIP_NOT_FOUND` as ADR-037 decision 4 planned, which the client has handled since CM-3. The guards dissolve: the trip module's legacy exemption, the new-world guard's content-half exemption and its old-world regex, the meta-test's dismantled list; and the trip module's internals drop `public` where the layout allows. Spring Modulith arrives with `verify()` green and each module's api package declared a named interface (ADR-039 decision 9). **The old root's sunset has one dated exception:** the eight diary-entry routes, now served from `postcard`.

**Events: none new.** The badge refetches; the fence reads the port synchronously. The first content event still waits for the FK-drop story.

**Docs.** The grilling record's PR already amended the glossary, ADR-037, ADR-039, the object contracts and the epic map. The gate annotates the epic-map line built, updates the BUILD_STATUS row, and records the measured counts.

## Testing Decisions

A good test here asserts **what a path answers, what rows exist afterward, and who may read** — never a module's internals. Four seams, all existing, confirmed by the founder at `/to-spec`; the first is written before anything is deleted.

1. **The HTTP integration-test seam**, the house's highest. Prior art: CM-1's contract classes, the publication contract test, the discovery filter and cursor tests, the private-author feed filter tests. Everything the story changes is proven here in the new modules **before** the old package's 39 tests are deleted, so the second PR's gate can diff assertion lines the way TW-1's did. Covered: the widened page read and the by-trip read; fork from the snapshot with provenance by the Itinerary's id; Discover's six routes over Itineraries, with the count agreeing with the list under every filter and fence; the feed's two scopes and its trip link; the profile's counts and showcase; the legacy diary adapters answering byte-identical from `postcard`; the port, so publish fences edits, membership and joining while unpublish, hard delete and a retired Itinerary unfreeze; a trip flagged by the old act with no Itinerary reading as unpublished and editable, seeded by raw SQL since no migration exists to step; and the ADR-034 case CM-1 lacked, a stranger reading a private owner's Itinerary, its photos and its fork, which fails on the tree today.
2. **The structural guards**, unit-level, backend and client. Prior art: the trip module's boundary test, the new-world boundary test, the module-guard meta-test, and the client's trip-grammar test from CM-3. The legacy exemption and the content-half exemption gone and their absence asserted; the dismantled list empty; `discovery`, `feed`, `profile` and `itinerary` under the rule; the cycle rule and Modulith's `verify()` green; the client repository naming no old-root path but the diary's.
3. **Playwright, both lanes.** Prior art: the suites CM-3 migrated. The publish, unpublish and fork walks on the new routes, the page by the Itinerary's id, a legacy trip-id link rewriting itself, Discover and Home over Itineraries; the old-root occurrences moved and the `--list` total read rather than the tick.
4. **The `dev` walk by hand, between the two PRs**, with `t1 = owner, t2 = stranger, t3 = private owner, t4 = member`: `t1` publishes and the workspace fences; `t2` reads the page, finds it on Discover and Home, forks it; `t3`'s Itinerary is readable by `t2`; `t1` archives and `t2` loses it; `t1` unpublishes, edits, republishes and the same link resurrects at the top of Discover; an old-flag trip of `t1`'s shows unpublished and editable, then one tap publishes it. Read against a backend whose log lines name the itinerary module.

Two things retire rather than get a seam: the twin test and the equivalence test, whose job ends when the old root does. The connection ceiling stands: every new integration test reuses the shared context signature.

## Comments

**2026-09-09 — sliced into thirteen tickets (`issues/`), and what the slicing found.** Two PRs as the spec says: tickets 01 to 08 are the first, 09 to 13 the second. *One structural fact reshaped the first PR:* Spring refuses two mappings on one route, so `discovery`, `feed` and `profile` cannot coexist with the old controllers on their unchanged paths — each surface is a **swap behind the path** inside the first PR, the new module landing with its tests and the old classes for that surface going in the same ticket, with the assertion lines carried over listed per ticket so the gate can diff them. "The old root still served" therefore holds for `/v1/itineraries/**` and the projection root, which the second PR removes, and not for the reader paths. *The edges:* 01 gates everything; 02 gates 03; 03 gates 04, 05, 06 and 07, which can run in any order; 08 gates the second PR, which is linear. *The founder's four rulings, confirmed at slicing:* TW-1's owed device walk folds into ticket 08; the client's query-key and hook rename stays out; the event publication registry rides ticket 12 only if 09 to 11 are green at its start; the Discover columns are title, destination, duration in days and cover URL, and trending keeps reading `publishedAt`. *On the founder's instruction* the spec and these tickets are this feature branch's initial commit rather than a docs PR; the spec's docs PR (#58) was closed unmerged.

**2026-09-09 — the two pre-work PRs are built and pushed, and what they found.** Both are branches off `dev`, not commits on this branch: `chore/cycle-rule-and-request-principal` and `chore/seven-owed-boundary-guards`, in that order (the second builds on the first's world only in spirit — they touch disjoint files and can merge in either order). Kept separate on the founder's call, so each stays revertable without the other and without this story. **Ticket 01's first AC still binds: both merge, then `dev` merges into this branch, before ticket 01's first change.**

*What the cycle rule found, and the one deferral it forced.* The request-principal move is `@CurrentTraveler`, `@AuthEmail` and `VerifiedContact` into `common.security` — 46 references across 42 files. The two argument resolvers **stay** in `identity.web`, because they resolve `identity`'s own `Traveler` and register it; that asymmetry is deliberate. `ModuleCycleTest` then measured the graph and found **six** 2-cycles, not the two ADR-039 anticipated: three were one file (`SecurityConfig` naming `JoinPaths`, `ReportPaths`, `WebSocketPaths`; the second PR moved those into `api`, which fixed the boundary breach but **not** the cycles — a boundary violation is reaching *behind* a front door, a cycle is the edge at any depth, and `SecurityConfig` still names all three), two are `identity` reaching `media` for avatars and `ws` for follow fan-out, and one is the old world being dismantled, which ticket 10 closes. Each is recorded with its reason and the ticket that closes it; the list can only shrink and a new cycle fails the build by name.

**The measurement that changed a plan, and the deferral the founder took.** `identity.Traveler` is a JPA `@Entity` named by **133 controller signatures across twelve modules**, and 38 files outside `identity` import it directly — so moving the annotations alone does *not* dissolve the cycle, because every controller still reaches behind `identity`'s front door for the entity itself. Of ~107 uses of the received traveler outside `identity`, **98 are `.id()`**; three are `displayName()`, two `avatarUrl()`, one `firebaseUid()`. The correct fix is a `common.security.RequestPrincipal` record that the resolver produces, leaving `Traveler` inside `identity` — but that is a wide refactor of 133 signatures in auth-adjacent code, not pre-work, so **the founder ruled it deferred** and the cycle rule records `identity <-> media` and `identity <-> ws` as known. **This is unowned work: it is not in this spec's Out of Scope list and no ticket claims it.** Ticket 12 is the natural home if it is to ride CM-5 at all; otherwise it needs its own line. Flagged rather than absorbed.

*What the guards found.* Not one of the seven owed modules still reaches `com.largata.itinerary` — TW-1's work already moved every `trip` reach onto `trip.api` — so the guards cost far less than the OWED list implied. One real breach existed (`JoinService` holding `InvitationService`, one method), closed by a one-method `invitation.api`. **Two guards deliberately do not enforce the contract rule:** `chat.api.ChatMessageResponse.of(ChatMessageView)` and `verification.api.VerificationCodeResponse.of(IssuedCode)` each map from an internal type in a static factory — real ADR-039 breaches, found by the guards on their first run. Reshaping two modules' published contracts is not the scope of the PR that first guards them, so each guard **asserts the breach still fails**, naming ticket 12: the day someone fixes it the assertion goes red and the real rule replaces it. **Ticket 12's "no exemption of any kind remains" AC must account for these two, or it cannot pass.**

*A trap worth keeping.* The first sabotage of the new guards **passed**, and the guard was right: an unused import is not a bytecode dependency, so ArchUnit cannot see it. A sabotage of a boundary guard must be a real usage — a field, parameter or return type — or it proves nothing. Unit suite 438 → 468 green across the two PRs.
